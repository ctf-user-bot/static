Verb agreement cheat sheet

Do / does / did — have / has / had — am / is / are / was / were. Quick reference plus common mistakes.

Do / does / did

Subject agreement

doI, you, we, they — "Do you like coffee?"
doeshe, she, it — "Does she work here?"
didany subject, past tense — "Did you call him?"

Note

Used mainly as an auxiliary for questions and negatives in present/past simple: "I don't know", "She doesn't work here", "They didn't come."

Oops — common mistakes

She don't knowShe doesn't know — "does" agrees with she/he/it, never "do."
Did she went?Did she go? — after "did," the main verb drops back to base form.
I didn't wentI didn't go — same rule, base form after "didn't."

Have / has / had

Subject agreement

haveI, you, we, they — "I have a car." / "They have finished."
hashe, she, it — "She has a car." / "He has finished."
hadany subject, past — "I had a car." / "They had finished."

Two jobs it does

Main verbPossession — "I have two brothers."
AuxiliaryPresent perfect — "I have finished the report."
AuxiliaryPast perfect — "I had finished before you called."

Watch out for

"have to / has to / had to" for obligation, and "had had" for past perfect of "have" as a main verb — "I had had breakfast before he arrived." Looks odd but is correct.

Oops — common mistakes

He have a carHe has a car — "has" for he/she/it, not "have."
I have wentI have gone — present perfect needs the past participle, not the simple past.
I haven't no moneyI haven't any money / I have no money — avoid double negatives in standard English.

Am / is / are / was / were

Present

amI — "I am ready."
ishe, she, it — "She is ready."
areyou, we, they — "They are ready."

Past

wasI, he, she, it — "I was ready." / "She was ready."
wereyou, we, they — "They were ready."

Note

Also works as an auxiliary for continuous tenses and passive voice: "I am working", "The cake was made by her."

Oops — common mistakes

There is many peopleThere are many people — "there is/are" agrees with the noun that follows, not with "there."
If I was youIf I were you — hypothetical/unreal conditionals use "were" for all subjects, including I/he/she/it.
Its rainingIt's raining — "it's" = it is/it has; "its" (no apostrophe) shows possession.

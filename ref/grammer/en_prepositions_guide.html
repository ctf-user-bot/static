Complete English Prepositions Cheat Sheet

What is a Preposition? A word that shows the relationship between a noun/pronoun and other words in a sentence. Prepositions answer questions like: Where? When? How? What direction?

LOCATION AND PLACE PREPOSITIONS

IN, ON, AT (Core Prepositions)

Preposition Use Examples
IN Inside enclosed spaces, cities, countries in the box, in Paris, in the room, in France
ON Surface, floor level, streets on the table, on 5th Avenue, on the floor, on the wall
AT Specific point, destination, location at home, at work, at the station, at the corner

BY, BESIDE, NEXT TO (Proximity)

Preposition Use Examples
BY Near, close to, beside (also means authored by) by the window, by the door, a book by Mark Twain
BESIDE At the side of something beside the river, sit beside me
NEXT TO Immediately adjacent to next to the park, next to the school
BY vs BESIDE vs NEXT TO: BY is general proximity. BESIDE and NEXT TO are more specific - at the side of something. NEXT TO is often slightly more distant than BESIDE.

UNDER, BELOW, BENEATH (Below Something)

Preposition Use Examples
UNDER Directly below, covered by, less than (age/amount) under the table, under 21, under the bridge, under the bed
BELOW Lower than, downstream, not reaching below the surface, below sea level, below average
BENEATH Under (formal), also means unworthy/unbecoming beneath the ground, beneath his dignity, beneath the stars
UNDER vs BELOW vs BENEATH: UNDER = directly under something. BELOW = lower in position or rank. BENEATH = under (formal) or unworthy of. Use UNDER most commonly.

OVER, ABOVE (Above Something)

Preposition Use Examples
OVER Above with contact/spanning, more than, finished over the bridge, over 30 people, the party is over
ABOVE Higher than, superior to, mentioned previously above the clouds, above 5000 feet, see diagram above
OVER vs ABOVE: OVER often implies crossing or covering. ABOVE just means higher position. "Over the mountain" (crossing it) vs "Above the mountain" (higher than it).

BETWEEN, AMONG (Multiple Items)

Preposition Use Examples
BETWEEN Two distinct items, space separating them between you and me, between two cities, between the lines
AMONG Three or more items, surrounded by among the trees, among my friends, among the crowd
BETWEEN vs AMONG: BETWEEN = two things or distinct items. AMONG = three or more things or a group. "Between the two of you" vs "Among the three of us".

MOVEMENT AND DIRECTION PREPOSITIONS

FROM, TO (Direction)

Preposition Use Examples
FROM Starting point, origin, source from London, from Monday, from my perspective, from the top
TO Destination, end point, direction to Paris, to tomorrow, to the store, to the left

INTO, ONTO, OUT OF (Directional Change)

Preposition Use Examples
INTO Movement to inside, transformation, continuation into the house, into the water, turn into a butterfly, get into trouble
ONTO Movement to on top of surface onto the bed, onto the floor, climb onto the roof
OUT OF Movement from inside to outside out of the house, out of control, out of the question
INTO vs ONTO: INTO = entering an enclosed space or changing state. ONTO = moving to on top of something.

THROUGH, ACROSS, PAST (Passing)

Preposition Use Examples
THROUGH From one side to other (inside), passing within through the forest, through the window, through the tunnel
ACROSS From one side to other (surface), crossing across the street, across the bridge, across the ocean
PAST Beyond a point, later than, by a certain time past the church, half past three, past midnight
THROUGH vs ACROSS: THROUGH = going inside and out the other side. ACROSS = going from one side to the other on a surface. "Through the forest" (inside) vs "Across the field" (over the surface).

TIME PREPOSITIONS

IN, ON, AT (Time)

Preposition Use Examples
IN Months, seasons, years, time of day, future duration in January, in summer, in 2025, in the morning, in 5 minutes
ON Specific days, dates on Monday, on July 4th, on Christmas, on my birthday
AT Exact times, night, dawn, specific moments at 3 PM, at noon, at midnight, at night, at dawn

DURING, WHILE (Time Period - Simultaneous)

Preposition Use Examples
DURING Throughout a period, happening within a time span during the meeting, during summer, during the war
WHILE At the same time as something else (often conjunction) while you were sleeping, while the sun shines (Wait while I call)
DURING vs WHILE: DURING introduces a time period. WHILE shows things happening simultaneously. "During the movie" (the whole time) vs "While I was watching" (at the same time).

FOR, SINCE (Duration)

Preposition Use Examples
FOR Length of time, duration (how long) for 5 hours, for 2 weeks, for a long time, for years
SINCE Starting point in the past, from a specific time (how long ago) since Monday, since 1990, since last year, since you left
FOR vs SINCE: FOR = duration (amount of time). SINCE = starting point. "I've worked here for 3 years" (duration) vs "I've worked here since 2022" (starting point). Use FOR with present perfect to measure time.

BEFORE, AFTER, UNTIL (Sequence)

Preposition Use Examples
BEFORE Earlier than, prior to, in front of before noon, before the meeting, before you go, before my eyes
AFTER Later than, following in time or sequence after the party, after 5 PM, after breakfast, after you
UNTIL Up to a certain time, continuing until that point until tomorrow, until Friday, wait until I call, until now

AGO (Past Time)

Preposition Use Examples
AGO Measured time in the past from now (always with past simple) two days ago, a year ago, long ago, 5 minutes ago
Note: Use AGO with past simple: "I arrived 5 minutes ago." NOT "I have arrived 5 minutes ago."

OTHER COMMON PREPOSITIONS

WITH, WITHOUT (Accompaniment/Manner)

Preposition Use Examples
WITH Accompanied by, using, having, against with my friend, with a knife, with a smile, argue with you
WITHOUT Not accompanied by, lacking, not using without you, without help, without sugar, without thinking

BY (Agent, Method, Transport)

Use Examples
Who did the action (passive voice) written by Shakespeare, painted by Picasso
Method or means by hand, by car, by email, by chance
Transportation by bus, by train, by plane, by boat
Measure or amount by the hour, by weight, by size

OF, FROM (Possession, Origin, Material)

Preposition Use Examples
OF Possession, belonging to, made from, about (topic) the house of John, a cup of coffee, a man of courage, speak of the future
FROM Origin, source, material, separation from France, made from wood, different from you, away from home
OF vs FROM: OF shows possession or what something contains. FROM shows origin or where something comes from. "A cup of coffee" (the cup contains coffee) vs "Made from beans" (the source material).

ABOUT, AROUND (Approximate, Surrounding)

Preposition Use Examples
ABOUT Topic/subject, approximately, roughly about history, about 50 people, talk about something, what is this about
AROUND Surrounding, approximately, nearby around the tree, around 5 PM, walking around, look around

PRO TIPS FOR USING PREPOSITIONS

Tip 1: Prepositions Follow Words, Not Rules
Many verb + preposition combinations are fixed phrases. You must learn them by memorizing: "interested in" (not "interested to"), "afraid of" (not "afraid from"), "proud of" (not "proud for"). When in doubt, check a dictionary.
Tip 2: Avoid Double Prepositions
Wrong: "Where is it at?" (wrong)
Correct: "Where is it?" (remove unnecessary preposition)
Common error: "off of" should be just "off"
Tip 3: Ending with Prepositions is OK
Old rule: Don't end sentences with prepositions (myth).
Modern English accepts: "What are you thinking about?" (natural)
Better than: "About what are you thinking?" (awkward)
Tip 4: Prepositions in Phrasal Verbs
Phrasal verbs have prepositions that change meaning:
- Look at (observe) vs Look for (search) vs Look after (care for)
Tip 5: Use Context to Choose
Multiple prepositions can work - choose based on meaning:
"At the beach" (specific location) vs "By the beach" (near it)
"On the beach" (standing on the sand) vs "In the water" (inside)

QUICK REFERENCE: COMMON PREPOSITIONS BY CATEGORY

Location: IN, ON, AT, BY, BESIDE, NEXT TO, UNDER, OVER, ABOVE, BELOW, BETWEEN, AMONG

IN (inside) | ON (surface) | AT (point) | BY (near) | BESIDE (at side) | NEXT TO (adjacent)
UNDER (below with contact) | OVER (above/crossing) | ABOVE (higher) | BELOW (lower)
BETWEEN (two things) | AMONG (three+ things)

Movement: FROM, TO, INTO, ONTO, OUT OF, THROUGH, ACROSS, PAST

FROM (origin) | TO (destination) | INTO (entering) | ONTO (on top of)
OUT OF (exiting) | THROUGH (inside passage) | ACROSS (surface passage) | PAST (beyond)

Time: IN, ON, AT, FOR, SINCE, DURING, WHILE, BEFORE, AFTER, UNTIL, AGO

IN (period) | ON (day) | AT (exact time) | FOR (duration) | SINCE (starting point)
DURING (within period) | WHILE (simultaneously) | BEFORE (earlier) | AFTER (later)
UNTIL (up to point) | AGO (past)

Other: WITH, WITHOUT, BY, OF, FROM, ABOUT, AROUND

WITH (accompaniment) | WITHOUT (lacking) | BY (agent/method) | OF (possession/topic)
FROM (origin) | ABOUT (topic/approximate) | AROUND (surrounding/approximate)
